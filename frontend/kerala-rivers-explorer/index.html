Kerala’s Rivers

Periyar · Nila · Pamba · backwaters

Kerala’s short, steep rivers drop from the Western Ghats to the Arabian Sea and the Kerala backwaters. This page covers the major ones: origin, districts, name, tributaries, and how they join the lagoons.

Major rivers

Origins, districts, names, tributaries

Select a river card to place it on the map. Most Kerala rivers flow west; a few (Kabini, Bhavani, Pambar) leave the state east toward the Kaveri.

Periyar

Origin
Sivagiri / Sundaramala hills, Western Ghats (Idukki)
Districts
Idukki, Ernakulam, Thrissur
Name
Periya aaru — “big river”; Kerala’s longest (~244 km)
Tributaries
Mullayar, Muthirapuzha, Cheruthoni, Edamala, Perinjankutty, Idamalayar
Backwaters
Reaches the Kochi estuary and Vembanad through branches around Aluva–Varappuzha

Bharathapuzha (Nila)

Origin
Anaimalai Hills (enters Kerala at the Palakkad Gap)
Districts
Palakkad, Malappuram, Thrissur
Name
River of Bharata; also called Nila
Tributaries
Gayathripuzha, Kannadipuzha, Kalpathipuzha, Thuthapuzha
Backwaters
Meets the sea at the Ponnani estuary — not the Vembanad system

Pamba

Origin
Pulachimalai, Peerumade hills (Idukki)
Districts
Idukki, Pathanamthitta, Kottayam, Alappuzha
Name
Pampa; the pilgrimage river of Sabarimala
Tributaries
Kakki, Kakkad, Aruvippuzha, Kallar
Backwaters
Kuttanad and Vembanad (Punnamada); snake-boat waters

Chaliyar

Origin
Elambaleri hills, Wayanad
Districts
Wayanad, Malappuram, Kozhikode
Name
Also the Beypore River at its mouth
Tributaries
Chalipuzha, Punnapuzha, Karimpuzha, Iruvanjippuzha
Backwaters
Beypore estuary on the Kozhikode coast

Chalakudy

Origin
Anamalai / Parambikulam hills
Districts
Thrissur, Ernakulam
Name
Named for Chalakudy town
Tributaries
Sholayar, Parambikulam; joins the Periyar at Elanthikkara
Backwaters
Via the Periyar to the Kochi–Vembanad mouth

Achankovil

Origin
Achankovil / Pasukida mettu, Western Ghats
Districts
Pathanamthitta, Kollam, Alappuzha
Name
From the Achenkovil settlement and shrine
Tributaries
Hill streams of the Achankovil valley
Backwaters
Joins the Pamba near Veeyapuram, then Vembanad

Kallada

Origin
Kulathupuzha hills, Western Ghats
Districts
Pathanamthitta, Kollam
Name
From the Kallada region of Kollam
Tributaries
Kulathupuzha, Chendurni
Backwaters
Feeds Ashtamudi Lake, a Ramsar backwater

Meenachil

Origin
Vagamon / Western Ghats fringe
Districts
Idukki, Kottayam
Name
From Meenachil taluk (meen, fish, is the usual folk etymology)
Tributaries
Small highland streams of Meenachil taluk
Backwaters
Enters Vembanad at Kumarakom
Farming

Agriculture

River water and backwater tides run Kerala’s wet-rice belts. Kuttanad (Pamba, Achankovil, and related channels) grows paddy below mean sea level behind bunds. The Palakkad Gap along the Bharathapuzha is a rain-shadow rice bowl. Pokkali rice in the Ernakulam backwaters uses Periyar–Vembanad salinity. Coconut, areca, banana, and pepper line the banks; the Periyar Valley Irrigation Project waters Idukki–Ernakulam fields.

Nature

Ecology

Headwaters sit in Western Ghats forest (Periyar Tiger Reserve on the Periyar; Shendurney on the Kallada). The Vembanad–Kol and Ashtamudi wetlands are Ramsar sites: mangroves, migratory birds, and brackish fisheries. Endemic hill-stream fishes live in Ghats tributaries. Floods (notably 2018) show how tightly settlement sits on these short catchments.
