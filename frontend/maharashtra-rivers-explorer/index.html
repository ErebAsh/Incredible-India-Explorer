Maharashtra’s Rivers

Western Ghats origins · Deccan plateau · Vidarbha

Most large rivers of Maharashtra rise on the Western Ghats and flow east across the Deccan to the Bay of Bengal. The Tapi is the main west-flowing exception. Select a river card to place it on the map.

Named rivers

Courses, tributaries, cities

Godavari

Course
Rises at Trimbakeshwar (Nashik) and crosses Marathwada before leaving for Telangana
Major tributaries
Pravara, Purna, Darna, Kadva, Sindphana; the Pranhita (Wardha–Wainganga) joins downstream of Maharashtra
Cities
Nashik, Paithan, Nanded

Krishna

Course
Rises at Mahabaleshwar (Satara) and flows through western Maharashtra into Karnataka
Major tributaries
Koyna, Yerla, Warna, Panchganga; the Bhima joins the Krishna in Karnataka
Cities
Wai, Karad, Sangli

Bhima

Course
Rises at Bhimashankar (Pune district) and runs the rain-shadow plateau toward Solapur
Major tributaries
Mula, Mutha, Indrayani, Ghod, Nira, Sina
Cities
Pune, Pandharpur, Solapur

Tapi (Tapti)

Course
Rises at Multai (Madhya Pradesh) and crosses Khandesh west to Gujarat and the Arabian Sea
Major tributaries
Purna, Girna, Panjhra, Waghur, Bori, Aner
Cities
Bhusawal, Jalgaon (Khandesh towns on or near the Tapi–Girna system)

Wardha

Course
Rises in the Satpura near Multai and drains western Vidarbha; meets the Wainganga to form the Pranhita
Major tributaries
Penganga, Wena
Cities
Wardha, Chandrapur (lower course)

Wainganga

Course
Rises in the Mahadeo Hills (Madhya Pradesh) and flows through eastern Vidarbha
Major tributaries
Kanhan, Pench, Bagh, Andhari
Cities
Gondia, Bhandara, Chandrapur
Farming

Agriculture

Canal and well irrigation on the Godavari, Krishna, and Bhima support the western Maharashtra sugarcane belt (Nashik–Ahmednagar–Pune–Sangli). Jayakwadi on the Godavari at Paithan is a major storage. The Bhima–Nira country is also drought-prone when the monsoon is weak. Khandesh along the Tapi and Girna grows cotton and banana. Vidarbha’s Wardha–Wainganga basin is cotton on the west and paddy in the wetter east (Gondia–Bhandara).
