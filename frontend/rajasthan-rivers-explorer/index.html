Named rivers

Origins, districts, names

Chambal

Origin
Janapav hills near Mhow (Madhya Pradesh); enters Rajasthan as a Yamuna tributary
Districts
Kota, Bundi, Sawai Madhopur, Karauli, Dholpur
Name
From the ancient Charmanvati (“river of hides”)

Banas

Origin
Khamnor hills of the Aravalli, near Kumbhalgarh (Rajsamand)
Districts
Rajsamand, Chittorgarh, Bhilwara, Tonk, Sawai Madhopur
Name
Often explained as van ki asha — “hope of the forest”

Luni

Origin
Nag Pahar / Pushkar valley, Ajmer (western Aravalli slope)
Districts
Ajmer, Nagaur, Pali, Jodhpur, Barmer, Jalore
Name
From Lavanavari — “salt river”; water turns saline downstream

Mahi

Origin
Vindhya hills (Madhya Pradesh); crosses southern Rajasthan toward the Gulf of Khambhat
Districts
Banswara, Dungarpur
Name
The Mahi of Mahi Kantha; also called Mahisagar in parts of its course

Sabarmati

Origin
Aravalli Range, Udaipur district; then flows through Gujarat to the Gulf of Khambhat
Districts
Udaipur (Rajasthan headwaters)
Name
From the older Svabhravati / Sabarmati of the Gujarat plain
Monsoon channels

Seasonal rivers

Most of Rajasthan’s drainage is monsoon-fed. The Chambal is the state’s most reliable perennial river. The Banas, Luni, Mahi, and Sabarmati swell in the southwest monsoon and shrink or go dry in the hot months, especially on sandy western plains.

West of the Aravallis, inland drainage dominates: the Luni ends in the Rann of Kutch rather than a delta, and tributaries such as the Jojari, Sukri, and Bandi run only after rain. In the north, the Ghaggar is a seasonal channel through Hanumangarh and Sri Ganganagar. Banganga and other eastern streams likewise depend on a few wet weeks. Tanks, khadins, and canal water matter because the natural rivers do not flow year-round.
